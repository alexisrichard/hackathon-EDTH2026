01

Brand foundation

One sentence: Heimdall watches every vessel in the theatre and tells scarce sensors where to look next — the horn sounds before the cable goes down.

Essence

The alarm before the battle. Heimdall is not the weapon and not the wall — he is the watch. The brand never celebrates destruction; it celebrates never being surprised.

Personality

Calm under threat. Severe, instrumental, precise. Speaks like a watch officer: short declaratives, evidence attached, zero exclamation marks. Confidence is shown by precision, not volume.

Audience

Defense operators and the people who fund them. NATO CUI Cell, navies, EMSA, cable owners, insurers. They distrust black boxes — every score the product shows is interpretable, and the brand inherits that: every claim, sourced.

Naming

Product

Heimdall — capital H, never "HeimdallAI", never "Heimdall.AI" in prose. The domain form heimdall.ai appears only as a URL or in the lockup.

The mark

The Gjallarhorn — when the mark needs a name (press kit, merch). Stripes = the signal; the box = the tasked objective; the stud = the watch post.

Feature language

The output is a cue (noun) and we task a sensor (verb). "Task-next queue", "tasked box", "cueing engine". Avoid "alert fatigue" words — Heimdall prioritizes, it doesn't nag.

Typography

TWO FAMILIES, TWO JOBS — NEVER A THIRD
Space Grotesk — the brand voice

Headlines, panel titles, vessel names, buttons-as-words, marketing. Weights 500–700. Wide tracking (+140 to +240) in caps; normal tracking in sentence case.

JetBrains Mono — the data voice

Everything that is measured: timestamps, MMSI/IMO, coordinates, scores, labels, table headers, code. Weights 400–700. If a value could appear in a log file, it's mono.

Pattern rules

The map is the hero

≥ 65% of the viewport. Panels never overlap the active cue. Chrome stays in Deep/Card surfaces; the void belongs to the theatre.

Cue boxes

Bracket frames in the recommended sensor's color, tag above-left (id · sensor · score · deadline), 2s opacity pulse. Never filled — the operator must see through them.

Vessel labels

Only when score ≥ watch, hovered, or pinned. Label = NAME · score, mono, scrim-backed. The sea must stay readable at zoom-out.

Criticality overlay

Teal→amber→red radial wash under everything, ≤ 16% alpha. It's context, not content — toggleable, never animated.

Time control

Scrubber always visible in replay. Red ticks = ground-truth incidents. Clock format YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SSZ everywhere, no exceptions.

Dark vessels

Radar-detected, AIS-silent contacts render as hollow glyphs (stroke only) in Breach Red — present but unaccounted for.

10

Voice & copy

A WATCH OFFICER, NOT A SALESMAN
Terse

Subject–verb–evidence. One sentence where one sentence works. No exclamation marks, ever — urgency is carried by the band color and the deadline, not punctuation.

Evidence-first

Every claim carries its number: "slowed to 2 kn over Estlink 2", not "behaving suspiciously". If the engine can't say why, it doesn't get to alert.

Unemotional about threat

We name vessels and behaviors, never nationalities or intent we can't prove. "Attribution: disputed" is a complete sentence.

Instead of → write

🚨 ALERT!! Suspicious vessel detected!!!EAGLE S · 0.91 · loitering over Estlink 2 · task SAR by 14:00Z
AI-powered next-gen maritime intelligenceWe tell your next satellite pass where to look.
Oops, something went wrong 😅AIS feed lost 13:42Z. Replaying cache. Live resumes automatically.

11

Do's & don'ts

DO

  • Let the void dominate — the map is the brand's living surface.
  • Use the mark as a status light (cyan → amber → red) in product chrome.
  • Keep every score next to its breakdown — interpretability is the moat.
  • Write timestamps in UTC with the Z, everywhere, including slides.
  • Use sensor colors only for tasking surfaces — they mean "go look here".
  • Carry the data attribution line on anything public.

DON'T

  • Round the mark's caps, rotate it, gradient it, or pair it with another icon.
  • Use Breach Red for anything that isn't an incident or destructive action.
  • Show a suspicion score as a percentage — it's a 0–1 score with a breakdown.
  • Add a third typeface, light-theme the ops surface, or skin the map "satellite-bright".
  • Say "spy", "hunt", or claim attribution we can't prove — we are the watch, not the verdict.
  • Animate chrome while vessels are moving — the data is the show.
12

Pitch deck pointers

LIGHT GUIDANCE — THE DEMO IS THE DECK

Principle: slides look like the product. Same Void background, same type pair, same band colors — the jury should not feel a context switch between the deck and the live demo. Slides carry one idea each; the demo carries the argument.

SLIDE 1 · THE THREAT
The Baltic incident map

Full-bleed dark map, five red ticks 2022→2025, one line: "Five cables in three years. Zero were predicted." Big mono dates.

SLIDE 2 · THE PROBLEM
"Defenders aren't lacking alerts. They're lacking prioritization."

One sentence, display type. The tip-and-cue gap: sensors are scarce; the ocean isn't.

SLIDE 3 · LIVE DEMO
Eagle S, Christmas Eve 2024

Cut to the product. Score climbs → SAR cue fires 13:35Z → cable cut 14:00Z. ~3 min, scripted, recorded backup video ready.

SLIDE 4 · HOW
The interpretable score

The §5.4 formula with the four term-bars from the alert card. Message: "auditable by an operator, not a black box."

SLIDE 5 · PROOF
Held-out validation

"Trained on N incidents. The model never saw Eagle S — and flagged it 25 minutes before the cut." One table, big numbers.

SLIDE 6 · ROADMAP / ASK
Today Baltic, tomorrow every theatre

North Sea → Atlantic → Pacific; AIS+SAR → +DAS +acoustic. Name the customer (NATO CUI Cell) and the ask.

Visual rules

Dark slides only. Full-bleed product screenshots, no browser chrome. Headlines in Space Grotesk; every number in JetBrains Mono. One accent color per slide.

The hero moment

Rehearse the 3 minutes until boring. The line to land: "We never showed the model this incident." Pause after it.

Hygiene

Data attribution line in the appendix (SOURCES.md string). UTC timestamps on screenshots. No "AI-powered" anywhere — show, don't adjective.
