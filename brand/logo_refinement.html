01

What makes "wifi" read — and the counter-moves

Kill the round caps

Wifi glyphs are round-capped, smooth, friendly. Every cut below uses butt/square terminals — machined edges, not pebbles.

Kill the dot

The dot-under-arcs is the wifi signature. Replaced with a square station stud — a sensor post, a hardpoint, a thing that's bolted down.

Give it a target

Wifi radiates at nothing. The Gjallarhorn radiates at the tasked box — bearing, sector, objective. That's the military grammar: signal with intent.

Recommendation

R4 · Chevron Cue. It answers both critiques at once: the chevrons make a wifi reading structurally impossible (no curves), and the tasked box keeps the cue gesture the team liked — now as the destination of the mark rather than a notch in it. It also picks up a second military echo for free: rank stripes. At 16 px it simplifies into a clean "stripes + box" silhouette.

R2 · Bearing is the alternate if the team prefers keeping the sonar-arc curvature — the bearing line through gapped range rings reads fire-control, not router. R1 is the fallback minimal change; R3 only if we'd rather drop the box entirely.
