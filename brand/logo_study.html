Logo Study — five marks for the watchman

Heimdall guards the Bifröst. He sees a hundred leagues, hears the grass grow, never sleeps — and when the breach comes, he sounds the Gjallarhorn first. Five logo directions, each anchored in one facet of the myth and one truth of the product.

00

What the mark must do

Survive 16 px

It lives as a favicon, map pin and topbar glyph before it ever lives on a slide. Geometry over detail.

Read as defense, not SaaS

The audience is NATO CUI / Marine Nationale–adjacent. Severe, instrumental, quiet confidence. No gradients-and-rounded-blob startup energy.

Carry the story

The pitch is tip-and-cue: we are the cheap continuous watch that tells scarce sensors where to look. The mark should be tellable in one sentence.

The five marks

C1GJALLARHORN★ Recommended

The myth: the horn Heimdall sounds when the enemy crosses — the alarm that precedes the battle, never follows it.

Three arcs swelling from a single listening post: at once a horn blast, a sonar ping and a radar sweep. It encodes the product's whole claim — we sound it before the cable goes down — in geometry a child could redraw. The asymmetric origin keeps it off the generic wifi-glyph axis; the variant notches a tasked satellite box into the outer arc, fusing alarm and cueing.

C2VÖRÐR — THE SLEEPLESS EYEHigh cliché risk

The myth: Heimdall needs less sleep than a bird and sees a hundred leagues, night or day.

A sensor slit on the sea horizon — eyelids as lens housing, a radar-gapped iris, the horizon line running through. Clean, scales well, instantly "surveillance." The problem is exactly that: the eye is the most-used glyph in the intelligence industry — and Palantir already owns the all-seeing-eye association in this market. We'd be decorating their lobby.

C3BIFRÖSTBest story · weakest at 16px

The myth: Heimdall's post is the Bifröst — the bridge between worlds. Nothing crosses unseen.

Three fading arcs span two landing stations; beneath them, a dotted submarine cable — the literal bridge between nations that we guard. The sentinel point hangs above the apex. It's the most precise metaphor of the five (our subject is protected links between two shores), and the rainbow-arc echoes sonar fans. Weakness: three thin arcs merge into mush at favicon size — it's a deck-and-letterhead mark, not a glyph.

C4HAGALL — THE RUNE★ Strong alternate

The myth: Hagall — the H-rune. In the younger futhark it's a six-armed star: hail, the hard strike from a clear sky.

Heimdall's initial, written the way the Norse wrote it. A six-armed star that is simultaneously a rune, a compass rose and a sensor-fusion node — six bearings converging on one point. This is the Anduril play: severe, angular, zero decoration, untouchable at 16 px. The elder-futhark ᚺ variant gives a second, more "monogram" cut for badges and stamps. Watch-outs: verify distance from any snowflake/asterisk trademark in the space, and it carries the least maritime flavor.

C5THE CUEProduct-literal · lives on as UI icon

The myth: none — this one is pure doctrine. A bearing, a swept sector, a tasked box on the ring.

The product's output drawn as an instrument face: "task your next pass on this box, on this bearing." Unmistakably ISR, reads instantly to a defense jury. As a company mark it's the least ownable — every targeting UI on earth has a reticle — and the five elements collapse below 24 px. Verdict: probably not the logo, but absolutely the iconography of the cueing overlay inside the app, whichever mark wins.

Recommendation

C1 · Gjallarhorn, in Bifröst Cyan, with the Space Grotesk wordmark. It's the only mark whose one-sentence explanation is the pitch: "Heimdall sounds the horn before the breach — we cue the satellite before the cable goes down." It scales like a wifi glyph (a proven 16 px geometry), doubles as a state-driven status light, and the cue-variant bakes the tasking box into the brand.

If the team wants maximum severity over maximum story, C4 · Hagall is the Anduril-grade alternate — and the two compose: Gjallarhorn as the product mark, the rune as a favicon/stamp/watermark second voice. Either way, C5's reticle becomes the in-app cueing icon, and C3 survives as a pitch-deck illustration.

Due diligence before locking: heimdall.ai domain availability + a trademark sweep ("Heimdall" is used in some cyber tooling — fine for a hackathon, check before incorporation).
